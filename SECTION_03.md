## Science edits and source basis

- Corrected the source typo “Alien” to “Aiden.”
- Replaced the broad claim that humans have larger organs with the narrower explanation that the human small intestine is much longer than the human large intestine, “small” means narrower, and the small intestine absorbs most nutrients. The [NIDDK digestive-system explanation](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works) supports the nutrient-absorption explanation.
- Used a roomier chimpanzee colon to illustrate its role in processing tough plant fiber. [Chivers and Hladik’s comparative morphology study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7441763/) addresses gut morphology including area, mass, and volume; it does not establish the source manuscript’s asserted species length ratios.
- Removed unsupported 4:1 human versus 1:1 chimp intestine-length ratios and all numeric measurements. The [UCSD CARTA small/large intestine reference](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/moca/topics/smalllarge-intestine-length-ratio) supports the qualitative adult human length distinction; adult measurements are not transferred to the child illustration.
- Labeled the diagram as a schematic, not to scale. It is a qualitative teaching diagram, not a measured species-anatomy comparison.
- Explicitly stated that digestion continues during play. Trevor’s nap is a story event, not a requirement for digestion.
- Used the qualified statement “Cooking softens many foods and helps us get energy from them.” [Carmody and colleagues’ 2011 primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065771/) found increased net energy from cooked meat and sweet potato in mice; the draft does not convert that result into a universal claim about all foods or humans.
- Preserved Aiden’s thanks to his parents for cooking and his offer to help next time.

## Final spread text

### S3P1 — Looks outside

> Aiden peeked out the window.
> There was Trevor in the little tree.
> He was already eating.
>
> Aiden smiled.
> “I know what to do first,” he said.

### S3P2 — Lucky lunch

> In the kitchen, Aiden’s Grandfather was making one of his favorite lunches: spaghetti and meatballs.
>
> Aiden twirled his noodles.
>
> Smack, smack, smack.
> Yum!
>
> Soon, his bowl was empty.
> Aiden rubbed his belly.
> “Now I’m ready to play!”

### S3P3 — Trevor rests

> Aiden hurried outside and climbed up the tree.
> Trevor was not eating anymore.
>
> But he was resting with his eyes closed, a big happy smile on his face, and both hands on his round primate belly.
>
> Aiden whispered, “Oh! Trevor’s done eating… but now he’s resting.”

### S3P4 — Aiden plays

> “I’ll let Trevor nap,” Aiden said softly.
>
> Then Aiden jumped down and ran off to play.
> He zoomed through the yard.
> He chased bugs.
> He collected leaves.
> He played all his favorite games.

### S3P5 — New question

> By the time Aiden got tired, Trevor was still resting.
>
> That evening Aiden told his parents,
> “I waited to play with Trevor until he was finished eating, but then he was napping. Why does he spend so much time resting?”

### S3P6 — Digestion and cooking

> Mom and Dad pulled down a picture.
>
> “We all need rest,” Mom said. “Digestion keeps going when we play, too.”
>
> She pointed. “Trevor’s roomy large intestine helps break down tough plant fiber.”
>
> “Our small intestine is much longer than our large intestine,” Dad said. “‘Small’ means narrower! It takes in most nutrients.”
>
> “Cooking softens many foods and helps us get energy from them,” Mom added.
>
> Aiden’s eyes widened.
> “Oh! So, cooking helps us all play and explore together. Thank you for spending time cooking for me. I want to help next time!”
>
> Mom and Dad laughed. “Sure, we’d love that!”
