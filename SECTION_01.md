## First-lesson science correction

The new S1P7 text separates large canine teeth and strong chewing muscles from the back teeth that crush and mash raw foods. The source explanation was corrected only in this production copy; the Google Doc is unchanged. Aiden's “chew forever” line is a child's question, not a factual claim that chimpanzees never tire.

The separate science review used [comparative canine-function research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ajpa.20779), [primate jaw-biomechanics research](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2021.0031), and [jaw-muscle fiber research](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1472946). Canine form is not presented as the mechanism for grinding food, and the lesson makes no categorical “never gets tired” claim. This copy review does not newly validate the inherited skull illustration's proportions.

Line wrapping is shown for readability and is not part of the wording changes.

### S1P1 — Introduction

> Aiden was a rambunctious boy who was visiting Hawaii. Rambunctious means full of energy and Aiden had plenty of it.

### S1P2 — Grandma teaching signs

> One day, Aiden saw a friendly chimpanzee outside his house. He remembered that chimpanzees don’t speak like people, but can learn signs. His grandmother had taught him sign language.

### S1P3 — New friend

> Aiden decided to make a new friend.
> 
> “I like to climb trees,” Aiden signed. “Want to play?”
> 
> Trevor pointed toward a little tree nearby.
> 
> Trevor nodded.
> 
> Up they went!

### S1P4 — Trevor eats

> They climbed from branch to branch until Trevor suddenly stopped.
> 
> He picked some fruit and pushed it into his mouth.
> 
> Kiss, kiss, kiss.
> His lips puckered and pulled.
> 
> Smack, smack, smack.
> His big jaws went to work.

### S1P5 — Tired cheeks

> Aiden grabbed some fruit too.
> 
> “Kiss, kiss, kiss!” said Aiden.
> 
> “Smack, smack, smack!”
> 
> At first, it was funny.
> 
> But Trevor kept chewing.
> And chewing.
> And chewing.
> 
> Aiden rubbed his cheeks.
> “My mouth is tired!”

### S1P6 — Question at home

> “This was fun, Trevor,”
> Aiden said, climbing down.
> “But I’m going home.
> Let’s play again tomorrow!”
> 
> At home, Aiden told his parents
> all about Trevor.
> 
> “Trevor can chew forever!
> Why doesn’t his mouth
> get tired?”

### S1P7 — First lesson

> Aiden’s parents showed him a picture of a chimpanzee skull beside a human skull.
> 
> “Chimpanzees have very large canine teeth and powerful chewing muscles,” his mother explained. “Their back teeth help crush and mash tough raw foods.”
> 
> Aiden touched his own jaw.
> 
> “So Trevor’s mouth is built for lots and lots of chewing?”
> 
> “Exactly.”
